ATO cents per km rates

The cents per kilometre method lets you claim a set rate for each business kilometre you drive in your own car. The rate covers fuel, servicing, registration, insurance and depreciation — you do not add those on top. The ATO sets the rate each year.

Income year Rate per km Maximum (5,000 km)
2026-2791 cents$4,550.00
2025-2688 cents$4,400.00
2024-2588 cents$4,400.00
2023-2485 cents$4,250.00
2022-2378 cents$3,900.00

How the cents per km method works

Take your business kilometres for the year, up to 5,000 per car, and multiply by the rate. If you drove 3,000 business kilometres in 2026-27, your deduction is 3,000 × $0.91 = $2,730.

You do not need receipts for the running costs. You do need to be able to show how you worked out your kilometres — a diary, a list of trips with dates and client names, or the myDeductions tool in the ATO app. You also need to show you own or lease the car.

The method is for sole traders and partnerships, and only for cars (vehicles that carry less than one tonne and fewer than 9 passengers). Travel between home and your usual workplace is private and does not count.

Cents per km or logbook method?

Cents per km is quick and needs no cost receipts, but it stops at 5,000 km. The logbook method has no kilometre limit. You keep a logbook for 12 weeks, work out your business use percentage, then claim that share of fuel, rego, insurance, servicing and depreciation. If you do a lot of driving for work, it often gives the bigger deduction.

Mileage calculator: frequently asked questions

  • The rate is 91 cents per business kilometre for the 2026-27 income year. It was 88 cents for 2025-26 and 2024-25. The calculator above uses the rate for the year you pick.
  • Multiply your business kilometres by the rate. For example, 3,000 km at 91 cents is $2,730. You can claim up to 5,000 km per car each year.
  • You can claim up to 5,000 business kilometres per car. At 91 cents that is a maximum of $4,550 for 2026-27. If you drive more than that for work, the logbook method may give you a bigger deduction.
  • You do not need receipts for the car running costs. The rate already covers fuel, servicing, rego, insurance and depreciation. You do need to show how you worked out your business kilometres, for example with a diary or the myDeductions tool in the ATO app.
  • Usually not. Travel between home and your regular workplace is private. If you run your business from home, a trip from home for business purposes, such as to a client, can count. Trips between client sites, to pick up supplies or to a second job can count too.
  • Yes. The 5,000 km limit applies to each car separately, and you can use a different method for each car. You can also switch methods from one year to the next.
